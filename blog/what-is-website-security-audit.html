What Is a Basic Website Security Audit?

A plain-English guide to understanding what a website security audit is, what it checks, and why your small business needs one.

Published: May 8, 2026 · Reading time: 8 minutes

If you own a website, you probably assume it is secure. You might have installed an SSL certificate, set a strong password, and maybe even added a security plugin if you are running WordPress. But here is the uncomfortable truth: most small business websites have security gaps that the owner never sees until something goes wrong.

A website security audit is the process of systematically checking your website for security weaknesses, misconfigurations, and exposures that could be exploited by attackers. Think of it like a home inspection before you buy a house — except instead of checking the foundation and roof, you are checking SSL certificates, security headers, exposed files, and known vulnerabilities.

What Does a Basic Website Audit Include?

A basic website audit is designed to be thorough without being overwhelming. At abcore pro security, our basic audit covers the following areas:

1. SSL/TLS Configuration

We check whether your SSL certificate is valid, properly installed, and configured with strong encryption settings. This includes checking the certificate expiry date, the certificate chain, supported TLS versions, and whether weak ciphers are enabled. Many site owners install a certificate and never check it again, only to discover it expired or was misconfigured months later.

2. Security Headers

Security headers tell browsers how to behave when interacting with your website. Missing headers like Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options, and Strict-Transport-Security can leave your site vulnerable to clickjacking, cross-site scripting (XSS), and man-in-the-middle attacks. Our audit checks for all standard security headers and explains which ones you are missing and why they matter.

3. Public Exposure Review

Your website may be revealing more information than you realize. Server version numbers, technology fingerprints, publicly accessible configuration files, and directory listings can all provide attackers with a roadmap of where to look for vulnerabilities. A basic audit scans for these exposures.

4. CMS and Plugin Detection

If your site runs on WordPress, Drupal, Joomla, or another content management system, we check whether your version is current and whether any known vulnerabilities exist in your CMS or visible plugins and themes. Outdated WordPress versions and plugins are among the most common ways small business websites get compromised.

5. Technical SEO and Health

While not strictly security-related, technical issues like broken pages, redirect chains, missing sitemaps, and indexing problems can affect your site's credibility and performance. Our audit includes a technical health check alongside the security review.

How Is a Basic Audit Different from a Penetration Test?

This is one of the most common questions we hear. A basic website security audit is primarily observational. We use automated tools and manual review to identify issues, misconfigurations, and exposures. We do not attempt to exploit vulnerabilities or break into your website. A penetration test, by contrast, involves actively trying to exploit vulnerabilities to prove they are real and assess their impact.

For most small businesses, a basic audit is the right place to start. It identifies the obvious problems — the ones that any casual attacker could find — and gives you a prioritized list of fixes. You can learn more about the differences in our post on website audit vs penetration test.

Why Small Businesses Need Website Security Audits

Small businesses are frequent targets of cyberattacks, not because they have valuable data worth targeting, but because they are often easy to compromise. Attackers use automated tools to scan thousands of websites for known vulnerabilities, and if your site shows up with an outdated WordPress version or a missing security header, it gets added to a list for exploitation.

Here is what a small business website security audit can prevent:

  • Website defacement — attackers replacing your homepage with their own content
  • SEO spam injection — malicious links and pages added to your site that hurt your search rankings
  • Data theft — customer information, contact forms, and login credentials being stolen
  • Malware distribution — your site being used to infect visitors with malware
  • Ransomware — attackers locking you out of your own website and demanding payment
