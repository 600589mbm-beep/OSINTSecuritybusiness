How Agencies Can Use Website Audits to Protect Clients

Web design and marketing agencies: add website security audits to your service stack and protect your clients from preventable breaches.

Published: May 8, 2026 · Reading time: 9 minutes

As a web design or marketing agency, you build and maintain websites for your clients. But when was the last time you checked those sites for security vulnerabilities? Most agencies focus on design, functionality, and performance — security often gets overlooked until something goes wrong. By then, the damage is done.

A multi-site audit approach lets agencies protect all their clients efficiently. And with white label options, you can offer security audits as your own service without building an in-house security team.

The Agency Security Challenge

Agencies face unique security challenges that individual website owners do not:

  • Scale: Managing security for 10, 20, or 50+ client websites is a significant undertaking
  • Blame: When a client's website gets hacked, they often blame the agency that built or maintains it
  • Legacy sites: Agencies often inherit websites built by others, with unknown security postures
  • CMS sprawl: Different clients use different CMS platforms, each with its own security considerations
  • Plugin management: Keeping track of plugin updates across dozens of WordPress sites is a logistical challenge

How Multi-Site Audits Work

Our agency website audit package is designed specifically for agencies managing multiple client sites. Instead of auditing sites one at a time, we audit all your clients in a batch and deliver:

This approach allows you to:

Typical Agency Scenarios

Scenario 1: The WordPress Maintenance Client

You manage 15 WordPress sites for various clients. You handle plugin updates and backups, but you have never done a comprehensive security audit. A multi-site audit reveals that 8 of the sites have outdated themes with known vulnerabilities, 3 have expired SSL certificates, and 5 are missing critical security headers. You fix these issues before any of them are compromised, and you present the audit findings to your clients as part of your proactive maintenance service.

Scenario 2: The New Client Onboarding

A new client asks you to take over their website. Before you agree, you order a multi-site audit (even if it is just one site initially) to understand the security posture. The audit reveals the site was compromised six months ago and still has backdoors in the theme files. You use this information to scope the cleanup properly and set the right expectations with the client.

Scenario 3: The Pre-Launch Check

You have just finished building a new website for a client. Before you launch it and hand over the keys, you run a security audit to ensure there are no obvious vulnerabilities. You find that the contact form plugin has a known vulnerability and the site is missing several security headers. You fix these before launch, preventing a potential breach on day one.

How Often Should Agencies Audit Client Sites?

Security is not a one-time event. We recommend:

  • Monthly: For high-value clients, e-commerce sites, or sites handling sensitive data
  • Quarterly: For standard brochure websites and small business sites
  • After any major change: New plugin, theme change, CMS update, or migration
  • Before ad campaigns: As covered in our pre-advertising security guide
