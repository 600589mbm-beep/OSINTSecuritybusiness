How to Prepare Your Website for a Security Audit

Everything you need to know before ordering a website security audit — authorization, scope, expectations, and how to get the most from your audit.

Published: May8, 2026 · Reading time: 9 minutes

Ordering a website security audit is a smart move for any business. But to get the most value from your audit, some preparation helps. This guide covers everything you need to know before, during, and after your audit — including the critical matter of authorization.

Authorization: The Most Important Step

Before anyone can scan your website for security issues, you must provide explicit authorization. Scanning a website without permission is illegal in most jurisdictions and violates the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) in the United States. This is why every legitimate security auditing service requires proof of authorization.

For our website audit preparation, we require one of the following:

  • You are the registered owner of the domain (verified via email associated with the domain registration)
  • You provide written authorization on company letterhead if you are an employee or contractor
  • For agency-managed sites, we require authorization from the domain owner

Our audit authorization process is straightforward and designed to protect both you and us. We never scan a domain without clear permission.

What to Prepare Before Your Audit

1. List the Domains and Subdomains

Decide exactly what you want scanned. Is it just the main domain (example.com)? Or do you also want subdomains scanned (app.example.com, shop.example.com, blog.example.com)? Each subdomain may require separate testing, so be clear about scope upfront.

2. Note Any Known Issues

If you are already aware of security concerns — a plugin you know is outdated, a certificate that needs renewal, a suspicious redirect — mention these when you submit your audit request. This helps us pay special attention to those areas and provides a baseline for measuring improvement.

3. Identify Critical Pages

While our automated scans crawl your site to discover pages, you can help by identifying your most important pages: homepage, login page, contact forms, checkout pages, and any custom application pages. This ensures they receive appropriate attention during the audit.

4. Check Your Hosting Restrictions

Some hosting providers have strict rate limiting or DDoS protection that may flag security scans as malicious traffic. If you know your hosting provider has such restrictions, let us know. We can adjust scan timing to be more gentle. Alternatively, check if your hosting provider requires advance notice before security testing.

5. Gather Team Access

After the audit, you will want to share the findings with your web developer, IT person, or hosting provider to implement fixes. Make sure you know who will be acting on the recommendations so you can forward the report to them promptly.

What Happens During the Audit

Understanding the process helps set expectations. Here is what happens during our basic website audit:

  1. Authorization verification: We confirm you have the right to request the audit
  2. Reconnaissance: We identify the technologies, server software, and CMS platform your site uses
  3. Automated scanning: We run SSLyze, testssl.sh, Nuclei, Nikto, WhatWeb, and other tools
  4. Manual review: We review scan results, filter false positives, and assess real risk
  5. Report writing: We create your plain-English report with risk-ranked findings
  6. Delivery: You receive the report and raw scan data, typically within 2-3 business days

Will the Audit Break My Website?

This is one of the most common concerns we hear. The answer is no — our basic audit uses non-destructive methods. We do not attempt to exploit vulnerabilities, inject payloads, or perform actions that could crash your site. The audit is read-only: we look, but we do not touch.

Even our Audit + Basic Pentest package uses careful, controlled testing techniques designed not to disrupt your website. However, because active testing is involved, we coordinate timing with you for that package.

What You Get After the Audit

Your audit deliverables include:

  • A PDF report written in plain English (no scare tactics, no jargon)
  • Findings ranked by risk level: Critical, High, Medium, Low, Info
  • Specific recommendations for each finding, with steps to fix
  • Raw scan data from every tool used, organized by tool
  • A summary page you can share with developers or hosting providers

After the Audit: Next Steps

Receiving the audit is just the beginning. Here is how to act on the findings:

  1. Start with Critical and High findings — these should be addressed within days
  2. Medium findings should be addressed within weeks
  3. Low and Info findings can be addressed during regular maintenance
  4. Share the report with your web developer or hosting provider for implementation
  5. Schedule a follow-up audit in 3-6 months to verify fixes

How Often Should You Audit?

Security is not a one-time event. We recommend:

  • New websites: Audit before launch
  • Active websites: Every 3-6 months
  • After major changes: New CMS version, new plugins, new functionality
  • Before ad campaigns: As covered in our pre-advertising guide
