Why Cheap Hosting Can Create Website Security Problems

Not all hosting is created equal. Understand the security risks of cheap hosting and how to choose secure website hosting for your business.

Published: May8, 2026 · Reading time: 10 minutes

When you are starting a business, every dollar counts. So when you see web hosting advertised for $2.95 per month, it is tempting to go with the cheapest option. But in the world of website hosting security, you often get exactly what you pay for — and the hidden costs can far exceed the monthly savings.

The Real Cost of Cheap Hosting

Cheap hosting providers keep prices low by cutting corners. They pack hundreds or thousands of websites onto a single server, use outdated software, skimp on security measures, and provide minimal support. While your website may load fine for visitors, the underlying security posture is often alarmingly weak.

Here are the most common cheap hosting risks we encounter when auditing websites:

1. Shared Hosting and the "Bad Neighbor" Effect

The cheapest hosting plans are almost always shared hosting — your website lives on a server with hundreds of other websites. If one of those sites gets compromised, the attacker may be able to move laterally to other sites on the same server. This is called the "bad neighbor" effect, and it means your security depends partly on strangers you will never meet.

We have seen cases where a compromised site on a shared server was used to attack every other site on that server, including legitimate business websites. The business owner did nothing wrong — they were just unlucky enough to share a server with a vulnerable neighbor.

For better secure website hosting, look for providers that offer isolated environments (like containers or virtual private servers) where each site has its own dedicated resources and security boundary.

2. Outdated Server Software

Cheap hosting providers often run outdated versions of PHP, Apache, Nginx, and other server software. They may be slow to apply security patches, or they may keep older versions available for "compatibility" — putting your site at risk.

Outdated server software is a leading cause of website compromises. When a vulnerability is disclosed in an old version of PHP or Apache, attackers scan for servers still running that version. Cheap hosting providers are overrepresented in these scans because they have many outdated servers.

3. No Server-Level Security Features

Quality hosting providers include security features like:

  • Web Application Firewall (WAF) to block common attacks
  • Malware scanning and removal
  • DDoS protection
  • Automatic backups with easy restoration
  • Isolated file systems between customer accounts
  • SSH access with key-based authentication

Cheap hosting rarely includes these features, or only offers them as expensive add-ons that cost more than the hosting itself.

4. Weak SSL/TLS Implementation

Many cheap hosts offer "free SSL certificates" through Let's Encrypt, which is great. But they often configure them poorly — enabling outdated TLS versions, using weak cipher suites, or failing to set up auto-renewal properly. We frequently find sites on cheap hosting with SSL configurations that would receive an "F" grade from SSL Labs.

A proper SSL/TLS configuration requires attention to detail that budget hosting providers often lack.

5. No Backup or Disaster Recovery

When was the last time your hosting provider tested a backup restore? Cheap hosting often includes "backups" that are incomplete, stored on the same server as your site (defeating the purpose), or simply not tested. When a site on cheap hosting gets hacked, the recovery process is often slow, expensive, or impossible.

6. Limited or No Support

Cheap hosting providers typically offer minimal support — often just a ticket system with slow response times and junior staff who can only follow scripts. When your site is compromised, you need help fast. With cheap hosting, you may wait days for a response while your site remains compromised.

7. Overselling and Performance Issues

To keep prices low, cheap hosts oversell their servers — promising more resources than they actually have. This leads to slow performance, which affects your search rankings and conversion rates. More importantly, overloaded servers are slower to apply security patches and may have instability that causes security features to fail.

How to Choose Secure Website Hosting

You do not need to spend hundreds per month for good security. There are reputable hosting providers at various price points that offer solid security. Here is what to look for:

  • Isolation: VPS or cloud hosting where your site is isolated from others
  • Current software: PHP 8.x, current Apache/Nginx, regular security patching
  • Security features: WAF, malware scanning, DDoS protection included
  • Backups: Daily automated backups stored offsite, with easy one-click restore
  • Support: 24/7 support with knowledgeable staff who can help with security issues
  • Reputation: Established providers with a track record of security responsibility

What About Managed WordPress Hosting?

Managed WordPress hosting (like WP Engine, Kinsta, or SiteGround) is often a good middle ground. These providers specialize in WordPress, keep the software updated, handle security at the server level, and provide WordPress-specific support. While more expensive than bare-bones shared hosting, they are significantly more secure and often include features that would cost extra elsewhere.
