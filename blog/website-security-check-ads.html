Why Your Website Needs a Security Check Before Running Ads

Spending money on advertising? Make sure your website is secure first — or you could be wasting your budget and damaging your brand.

Published: May 8, 2026 · Reading time: 8 minutes

You have set your advertising budget, written compelling ad copy, and identified your target audience. But before you hit "launch" on that Google Ads or Facebook Ads campaign, there is one critical step many businesses skip: a website security check.

Running ads to an insecure website is like pouring water into a bucket with holes. You are paying to drive traffic to a site that may be compromised, damaging your brand reputation and wasting your advertising spend.

The Real Cost of Advertising to an Insecure Site

When you run ads, you are paying for every click. If your website has security issues, those clicks may be worthless or actively harmful:

1. Ad Networks Will Block Your Site

Google Ads, Bing Ads, and Facebook Ads all scan landing pages for malware, phishing content, and suspicious behavior. If your site is compromised — even if you did not know it — your ads can be disapproved, your account can be suspended, and your ad spend can be forfeited. A website vulnerability scan before launching ads helps you avoid this scenario entirely.

2. Visitors See Security Warnings

Nothing kills conversion rates faster than a browser security warning. If your SSL certificate has expired, your site serves malware, or your domain is blacklisted, browsers will display prominent warnings telling visitors to go back. Even a small percentage of lost conversions can destroy the ROI of your ad campaign.

3. Compromised Sites Redirect Traffic

One of the most common results of a website compromise is unauthorized redirects. Attackers often inject code that redirects visitors from your site to scam pages, gambling sites, or competitor websites. If you are paying for those clicks, you are literally paying to send customers away.

4. SEO Spam Destroys Landing Pages

Many website compromises involve injecting SEO spam — hidden links and pages designed to boost the attacker's search rankings. This spam can appear on your landing pages, making them look unprofessional or causing ad networks to reject them. A pre-advertising security check catches this before you start spending.

5. Stolen Lead Data

If your website has contact forms or collects leads, a security vulnerability could allow attackers to intercept that data. You are paying for advertising to generate leads, but those leads may be stolen or modified before they reach you. Worse, you could be violating data protection regulations if customer data is compromised.

What a Pre-Advertising Security Check Covers

A proper website security check before launching ads should include:

  • Malware scan: Check if your site is already infected with malware or blacklisted by Google Safe Browsing
  • SSL/TLS verification: Ensure your certificate is valid, properly installed, and will not expire during your campaign
  • Redirect check: Verify that your landing pages load correctly without unauthorized redirects
  • Form security: Check that contact forms and lead capture pages are not vulnerable to injection or interception
  • Page integrity: Scan for unauthorized script injections, hidden iframes, or modified content
  • Performance check: Slow or broken pages hurt ad conversion rates — a technical health check ensures your pages load properly

Real-World Example: The Hidden Compromise

Consider a real scenario we encountered: A local service business was running Google Ads spending $3,000 per month. Their website had been compromised six months earlier with an SEO spam injection that was invisible to the naked eye — the spam was hidden in the page source and only displayed to search engine crawlers. Their ads were running fine initially, but Google eventually detected the spam and disapproved their ads. They lost two weeks of advertising time and had to spend additional money cleaning up the site before ads could resume.

A $199 security audit before launching the campaign would have caught the compromise immediately.

Timing Your Security Check

The best time to run a website vulnerability scan is 3 to 5 days before you plan to launch your advertising campaign. This gives you time to fix any issues that are found without delaying your launch. Our basic audit has a 2-3 business day turnaround, making it a perfect fit for pre-launch preparation.

Ad Platform Security Requirements

Different advertising platforms have different security requirements:

  • Google Ads: Requires HTTPS for all landing pages. Scans for malware, phishing, and deceptive practices. Will flag sites with sudden changes in content or structure.
  • Facebook/Meta Ads: Checks for malware and policy violations. Sites with poor security or suspicious redirects are rejected.
  • LinkedIn Ads: Requires secure landing pages. Reviews sites for professional standards and security compliance.
  • Microsoft Ads: Similar to Google, with strict malware and phishing detection.
