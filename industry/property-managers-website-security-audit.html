Property Managers Handle Massive Data Volumes

Tenant applications, lease agreements, maintenance requests, and vendor contracts all flow through your websites. Is that data secure?

Property management companies operate at a scale that creates unique security challenges. A single firm might manage 50 properties, each with its own website or page, a central tenant portal, a vendor portal, and an applications system. Each of these digital assets represents an entry point for bad actors. We've found property management websites with tenant applications sitting in publicly accessible directories, vendor lists exposing payment terms, and maintenance portals with default passwords still in place.

The stakes are high. Tenant data includes Social Security numbers, income verification, credit reports, and personal references. Property details reveal ownership information, financial terms, and operational data that competitors and scammers actively seek. When you manage portfolios across multiple markets, your attack surface multiplies with each additional property website.

Property Management Website Risks

🏢

Tenant Portal Vulnerabilities

Resident portals with weak authentication, payment systems without proper encryption, and maintenance request forms that expose unit numbers and personal details.

📋

Application Data Exposure

Online rental applications storing data in plain text, PDF applications in public directories, and form submissions without encryption during transmission.

🤝

Vendor Portal Risks

Contractor portals with excessive access, vendor lists revealing payment terms, and work order systems that leak property addresses and scope details.

🏘️

Multi-Property Exposure

Each property site may have different security standards. One vulnerable property website can provide a path to your entire portfolio's data.

💳

Payment Security Gaps

Rent payment pages without proper SSL, third-party payment widgets with inadequate security, and receipt systems that expose financial data.

🔍

Lease Document Leaks

Sample leases, tenant handbooks, and policy documents stored in accessible directories. These files often contain more information than intended for public view.
