Client Confidentiality Starts With Your Website

Law firms are high-value targets for data breaches. Your website often holds more sensitive information than you realize.

Attorney-client privilege extends to your digital presence. When your law firm website has security gaps, you're not just risking a data breach — you're potentially violating ethical obligations and state bar requirements. We've audited law firm websites that exposed client names in public case results, published settlement amounts that should have remained confidential, and left intake forms vulnerable to data interception.

Many law firms rely on WordPress for its content management flexibility, but legal websites are disproportionately targeted by automated attacks. Outdated plugins, weak login protection, and missing security headers create entry points that threaten client data. A single compromised law firm website can expose thousands of client records, creating malpractice exposure that no insurance policy fully covers.

Legal Website Vulnerabilities We Find

⚖️

Intake Form Risks

Contact forms and case intake systems without proper encryption or validation. Client details submitted in plaintext create serious confidentiality risks.

🔐

WordPress Vulnerabilities

Legal themes and plugins with known exploits. Outdated WordPress cores on law firm sites are among the most commonly compromised website types.

📋

Attorney Profile Exposure

Excessive personal information in attorney bios, publicly listed direct emails, and staff directories that enable targeted phishing campaigns.

📁

Case Information Leaks

Public case results that reveal more than intended, client testimonials with identifiable details, and settlement information that should remain private.

🔍

Document Exposure

PDF resources, legal guides, and client materials stored in publicly accessible directories. Search-indexed documents containing sensitive references.

🛡️

Missing Security Headers

No CSP, missing HSTS, and absent X-Frame-Options. These missing protections leave visitors vulnerable to clickjacking and data interception.

Why Law Firms Need Specialized Audits

Ethical Compliance Requirements

State bar associations increasingly expect reasonable cybersecurity measures. A documented website security audit demonstrates due diligence. Our reports include raw scan data that your ethics counsel or IT team can review, giving you defensible evidence of your security efforts.

Protect Client Intake Channels

Potential clients submit highly sensitive information through your website contact forms. Practice areas like family law, criminal defense, and personal injury involve confidential details shared before an attorney-client relationship is even established. We test your intake forms for basic security flaws that could expose this data.

Multi-Attorney Practices

Larger firms often maintain separate profile pages, practice area sections, and attorney-specific content. Each page represents a potential attack surface. Our Audit + Basic Pentest package performs deeper crawling to review these extended footprints.

Third-Party Integrations

Legal practice management tools, client portals, and marketing integrations often load scripts on your website. We review these third-party connections for privacy and security implications that could affect client data handling.
