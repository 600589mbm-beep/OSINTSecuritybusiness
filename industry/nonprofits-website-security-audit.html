Nonprofits Are Targets Too

Charities process donations, store donor data, and manage volunteer information. Limited IT budgets make nonprofits attractive targets for attackers.

Nonprofit organizations face a difficult paradox: you're entrusted with donor credit card data, personal information, and financial records, but you likely have the smallest IT budget to protect it. We've audited nonprofit websites with donation pages submitting data without encryption, donor databases partially exposed through website vulnerabilities, and volunteer signup forms collecting Social Security numbers with no security measures in place.

The consequences of a breach go beyond financial loss. When donors lose trust, fundraising suffers. When a charity's website is compromised, donors see it as a failure of stewardship. Grant makers may question your organizational competence. Board members may face scrutiny. A single security incident can unravel years of reputation building. Yet most nonprofits operate on the assumption that "we're too small to be targeted" — exactly the mindset attackers count on.

Nonprofit Website Risks

💝

Donation Page Vulnerabilities

Donation forms without proper SSL, payment data submitted insecurely, and third-party donation widgets with inadequate security controls.

👥

Donor Data Exposure

Donor databases accessible through website vulnerabilities, donation confirmation pages revealing amounts, and thank-you pages exposing donor names.

🤝

Volunteer Signup Risks

Volunteer forms requesting addresses, phone numbers, and background info — all submitted through unsecured forms or stored in publicly accessible locations.

📧

Email & Contact Exposure

Staff email addresses listed publicly, board member contact info exposed, and "info@" addresses that become phishing targets for your donor base.

🔐

Outdated CMS Platforms

WordPress sites running on minimal budgets with no maintenance plan. Outdated plugins, abandoned themes, and known vulnerabilities that attackers scan for automatically.

📁

Public Document Risks

Annual reports, donor lists, grant applications, and tax documents stored in publicly accessible directories. Search-indexed files containing donor names and amounts.
