Churches Collect More Data Than You Realize

Member directories, donation records, volunteer signups, and event registrations all flow through your church website. Is that data secure?

Churches occupy a unique position: you collect deeply personal information about your congregation — addresses, phone numbers, family details, giving history, and prayer requests — but you often have the smallest IT budget of any organization type. Many churches rely on volunteers to manage their websites, and when those volunteers move on, websites get neglected. We've found church websites with member directories fully exposed to the public, online giving pages without proper encryption, and volunteer forms collecting sensitive data through unsecured connections.

The trust relationship between a church and its members is sacred. When a church website is compromised, it's not just a technical problem — it's a breach of that trust. Donors who see security warnings on your giving page will take their tithes elsewhere. Members whose personal information is exposed will hesitate to share prayer requests or participate in ministries. A secure website supports your mission rather than undermining it.

Church Website Vulnerabilities

🙏

Online Giving Security

Donation pages without proper SSL, giving widgets with inadequate security, and payment forms that submit data without encryption. Browser warnings scare donors away.

👥

Member Directory Exposure

Church member directories accessible to anyone with the link, profile pages revealing personal details, and login-protected pages with weak authentication.

📋

Volunteer Registration Risks

Volunteer signup forms requesting background info, ministry participation sheets with personal data, and event registrations stored without protection.

📧

Staff Email Exposure

Pastor and staff email addresses listed publicly become phishing targets. Compromised church emails are used to scam congregation members with fake urgent requests.

🔐

Outdated Website Platforms

Church WordPress sites managed by volunteers who may not understand security. Outdated plugins, old themes, and forgotten admin accounts with weak passwords.

📁

Public Document Risks

Sermon notes, member newsletters, and church financial reports stored in publicly accessible directories. PDF files containing member names and donation appeals.

Why Churches Need Website Security

Protect the Offering

Online giving is now a primary revenue source for most churches. When donors see security warnings or experience payment issues, they give less or stop altogether. Our audit identifies the technical issues that silently suppress online giving — missing HSTS, mixed content warnings, and vulnerable giving widgets. Fix these issues and remove friction from the donation experience.

Multi-Campus and Multi-Site Needs

Many churches operate multiple campuses, each with its own website or section. Our Multi-Site Audit covers your entire digital presence, from the main church site to campus pages to ministry-specific microsites. Get a consolidated view of security across your entire church web presence.

Volunteer and Small Staff Limitations

We understand that most churches don't have dedicated IT staff. Our plain-English reports are written for non-technical readers — your pastor, your board, and your volunteer web coordinator can all understand the findings and recommended actions. The raw data is there for when you bring in technical help.

Stewardship and Compliance

Church boards have fiduciary responsibility for member data and donation records. Our audit report provides documented evidence of your security efforts. Share it with your board, your insurance provider, and your denomination's leadership to demonstrate responsible stewardship of digital assets.
