Patient Data Deserves Dental-Grade Security

Dental practices collect sensitive patient information through websites every day. Is your online presence putting PHI at risk?

Dental offices face a unique challenge: you need to be accessible online for new patient acquisition, appointment scheduling, and patient communication — but every digital touchpoint is a potential HIPAA concern. We routinely find dental websites with patient intake forms that submit data without encryption, online booking systems with weak authentication, and patient portals that expose more than intended.

Unlike general medical practices, dental offices often rely on smaller IT providers and budget-conscious web management. This creates a perfect storm: high-value patient data, regulatory compliance requirements, and often outdated or poorly maintained website infrastructure. A single exposed patient record can trigger notification requirements, regulatory scrutiny, and reputational damage that takes years to repair.

Dental Website Risks We Discover

🦷

Online Booking Vulnerabilities

Appointment scheduling systems with weak security, patient data submitted without encryption, and booking widgets that leak information to third parties.

📋

Patient Intake Forms

New patient forms requesting medical history, insurance details, and personal data — all submitted through unsecured or poorly configured web forms.

🔐

Patient Portal Exposure

Dental portal login pages with missing security headers, weak password policies, and session management issues that could compromise patient accounts.

📁

PHI in Public Pages

Patient testimonials with identifiable details, before-and-after photos with metadata, and case studies that reveal more than HIPAA allows.

🛡️

HIPAA Web Compliance

Missing privacy notices, inadequate terms of use, third-party script review for PHI handling, and encryption gaps on data transmission.

🔍

Staff Information Leaks

Directory pages listing doctors' personal emails, staff bios with excessive detail, and contact information that enables targeted attacks.

HIPAA Considerations for Dental Websites

What HIPAA Means for Your Website

While your practice management software likely has HIPAA compliance controls, your website often falls through the cracks. Patient forms, appointment requests, and contact submissions all transmit PHI. Our audit identifies where your website may be creating compliance gaps, giving you a roadmap to address them before they become regulatory issues.

Multi-Location Dental Practices

Dental groups with multiple locations often maintain separate websites or location-specific pages. Each site needs individual review. Our Multi-Site Audit package covers your entire practice group, with a summary dashboard showing your overall security posture across all locations.

Third-Party Booking Integrations

Most dental websites rely on third-party booking platforms like Zocdoc, RateMDs, or custom scheduling widgets. We review how these integrations handle patient data, what information is shared, and whether proper encryption is in place during transmission.

Insurance and Liability

Dental malpractice insurers increasingly ask about cybersecurity measures. Our audit report, complete with raw scan data, provides documented evidence of your proactive security efforts. Share it with your insurance provider to demonstrate due diligence.
